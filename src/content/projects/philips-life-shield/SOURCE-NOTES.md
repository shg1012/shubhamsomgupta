## External claim sources

### Poor-quality-care mortality

The project deck's approximately 4,300 deaths-per-day figure is consistent with 1,599,870 annual deaths in India attributed to use of poor-quality services:

- Margaret E. Kruk et al., "Mortality due to low-quality health systems in the universal health coverage era: a systematic analysis of amenable deaths in 137 countries," 2018: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6238021/
- NHSRC presentation reproducing the country table and citing Kruk et al.: https://qps.nhsrcindia.org/sites/default/files/2022-03/Overview%20Dr%20J%20N%20S%20.pdf

1,599,870 divided by 365 is approximately 4,383. The deck's 4,300 value is therefore treated as a rounded historical estimate based on 2016 data.

### Emergency-care delay

The 30% mortality figure associated with delayed emergency care appears in:

- NITI Aayog and AIIMS, "A Country Level Assessment of Current Status of Emergency and Injury Care at Secondary and Tertiary Level Centres in India": https://www.niti.gov.in/sites/default/files/2023-03/A%20COUNTRY%20LEVEL%20ASSESSMENT%20OF%20CURRENT%20STATUS%20OF%20EMERGENCY%20AND%20INJURY%20CARE%20AT%20SECONDARY%20AND%20TERTIARY%20LEVEL%20CENTRES%20IN%20INDIA.pdf

The deck's under-five-minute emergency-ward value could not be matched to a recoverable authoritative source. It is labelled as the project's research target, not as a universal clinical standard.

### COVID-19 figure

The 254 deaths-per-day value is present on page 2 of the original 2020 deck. Its snapshot date and source were not included and could not be recovered. It is retained only as a dated project-research figure.

### BRD Medical College

Page 4 attributes 63 child deaths to an oxygen-cylinder shortage. The number remains visible because the user asked to preserve the deck's numbers, but the public copy notes that the causal claim was disputed:

- DD News contemporaneous report carrying the state government's position: https://ddinews.gov.in/national/govt-suspends-principal-brd-medical-college

### Crash-cart organisation

Page 40 links to:

- https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5847507/

The case study reports the drawer organisation as a concept proposal informed by the linked source, not as a clinically approved configuration.

- A physical prototype
- Participants, test protocol, tasks, findings, or usability metrics

- Engineering verification
- Materials, CMF specifications, production processes, tolerances, BOM, costing, or suppliers
- Formal concept-selection criteria
- Patents, implementation, launch, adoption, or measured impact

Final clinical scenes are explicitly treated as conceptual visualisations.
